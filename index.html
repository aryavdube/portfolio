04 / RESEARCH PAPERSCROLL THE PAPER ↓
YOUNG SCIENTIST JOURNAL / RESEARCH ARTICLE

Enhancing AAC Systems Through Artificial Intelligence

Aryav Dube · Parent and family perspectives on limitations and solutions

SCROLL TO READ THE STUDY ↓

01 / ABSTRACT

Where AAC systems fall short.

This 11-week mixed-methods study examines limitations families encounter with augmentative and alternative communication systems and explores how AI could make them more adaptive and expressive.

02 / METHODS

Parent and family interviews were analyzed for recurring themes about device use, communication needs, and possible improvements.

03 / FINDINGS

Seven barriers to better communication.

Recurring challenges included difficult navigation, limited symbols, linguistic support, practice opportunities, emotional expression, social fluency, and strategic communication.

04 / CONCLUSION

Personalization, prediction, and context-aware interfaces may help AAC tools better support communication, independence, and inclusion. Further research is needed.

02 / THE PERSON BEHIND THE WORK

Curiosity with
intention.

AD.

I'm Aryav Dube, a UCLA Linguistics and Computer Science student in the Class of 2029, shaped by the Bay Area and now based in Los Angeles. My interest in technology didn't begin with a screen. It began in special education classrooms, where I spent thousands of hours learning from students whose needs too often get treated as an afterthought.

That experience changed the questions I ask when I build. Could a learning tool adapt to the student instead of the reverse? Could an interface make communication easier, not more complicated? SpecialThinkers and MindLuminary grew from those questions, alongside research into assistive communication and the human side of AI.

Today, I'm continuing that work through research at UCLA and by building products for communities beyond the classroom. I care about the details of design and code, but even more about whether the result gives someone greater access, agency, or a sense of belonging.
